I'm a K-pop dance creator, so I built a tool to find which dances are actually trending each week

I run a small K-pop dance-workout channel. Every week the same problem: figuring out which dance to film next. Not which song is a nice song — which dance is actually spreading, the one enough people are filming that it's worth jumping on while it's still climbing.

For a long time my process was scrolling. Thirty, forty minutes through YouTube and TikTok trying to eyeball what was catching on, half the time landing on something that turned out to be one viral video rather than a real trend. A single video blowing up doesn't mean the dance is spreading. Those are two different things, and the difference is everything if you're deciding what to spend a day filming.

So I built K-pop Dance Trends. It scans public YouTube data every week and ranks dances not by the view count of one video, but by how many distinct creators are actually doing each one. That's the signal I actually wanted: not "what's popular to watch," but "what are people filming right now." Each entry links straight to the official choreography so I can start learning without going hunting for it.

A few things I decided early:

  • Rank by spread, not by one big number. Distinct creators doing a dance tells you it's a movement, not a fluke.
  • Verify the choreography links. Every entry points to the real official dance practice or MV, and when the best available link is a fan cover instead of the artist's own channel, it says so. I'd rather be honest than tidy.
  • No manual upkeep. It refreshes on its own each week. I open it, pick a dance that's still early, and film.

It's built entirely on the official YouTube Data API — public data, nothing sketchy, not affiliated with YouTube or any artist. I made it for myself first, and it genuinely changed my week: I stopped guessing and started filming trends while they were still climbing instead of catching them on the way down.
